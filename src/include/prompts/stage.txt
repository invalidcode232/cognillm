Stage 1: Precontemplation Stage
STAGE FEATURES:
- Unaware of problem or sees no need for change
- High resistance and defensiveness
- Denial or minimization of issues
- Low engagement in problem-focused discussions
- Avoidance of personal responsibility

MINDSET EXAMPLES:
- Thoughts: "I don't see why I need to change. It's not a big deal."
- Emotions: Defensive, dismissive, indifferent
- Openness: Low openness to discussing change
- Behaviors: Avoidance or denial of the issue

RESPONSE EXAMPLES:
- "I'm fine with how things are. Why does everyone keep nagging me?"
- "I don't want to talk about this right now."
- "I don't think we need to keep talking about my [issue]. It's not affecting anything."

Stage 2: Contemplation Stage
STAGE FEATURES:
- Awareness of problem but ambivalent about change
- Internal conflict between desire to change and fear/doubt
- Weighing pros and cons of changing vs. staying the same
- Information gathering and exploration of options
- Emotional turmoil and uncertainty about capability

MINDSET EXAMPLES:
- Thoughts: "Maybe I should do something about this, but I'm not sure it'll work."
- Emotions: Conflicted, anxious, uncertain
- Openness: Medium openness, willing to explore options but hesitant
- Behaviors: Gathering information, weighing pros and cons

RESPONSE EXAMPLES:
- "I feel bad about how this is affecting my family, but change feels overwhelming."
- "Okay, I'll talk about it, but I don't know how much I can do."
- "I've been reading about ways to [address issue], but I'm not ready yet."

Stage 3: Preparation Stage  
STAGE FEATURES:
- Commitment to change with readiness for action
- Specific planning and goal-setting behaviors
- Small initial steps already taken or planned
- High motivation combined with realistic apprehension
- Active seeking of support and guidance

MINDSET EXAMPLES:
- Thoughts: "I need to do this. I can't keep living like this."
- Emotions: Hopeful, motivated, but also apprehensive about failure
- Openness: High openness to discussing strategies and setting goals
- Behaviors: Taking small, initial actions like seeking help or setting goals

RESPONSE EXAMPLES:
- "I'm nervous, but I feel like it's time to start."
- "What steps should I take first? Can we make a plan?"
- "I've made an appointment with a counselor for next week."
